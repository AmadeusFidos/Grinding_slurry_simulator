
==============================================================================
  LAPIDARY SLURRY SIMULATOR
==============================================================================

Stone:       Agate / chalcedony
Lap:         Float glass plate
Pressure:    100 kPa (40 N over 400 mm2)
Stroke:      0.25 m/s
Room:        21 C, 45 % RH
On the plate: 8 ml spread over 200 cm2

==============================================================================
  RECOMMENDED RECIPES  (one per grade you own)
==============================================================================

--- Black silicon carbide 320 (d50 29.2 um) ---
For 100 ml of Black silicon carbide 320 slurry:
      25.9 g   grit
      29.4 ml  water
      62.4 ml  glycerine
         2     drops of dish soap
  (19 wt% solids, 73 wt% glycerine in the liquid)
  Still works between 2 and 63 wt% grit, and between 66 and 90 % glycerine (each varied on its own).
  Expect 35 um/min, Ra 0.75 um, like single cream.
  Tightest limit: will not crust (margin +0.82).

--- Black silicon carbide 400 (d50 17.3 um) ---
For 100 ml of Black silicon carbide 400 slurry:
      16.8 g   grit
      30.4 ml  water
      64.4 ml  glycerine
         1     drop of dish soap
  (13 wt% solids, 73 wt% glycerine in the liquid)
  Still works between 3 and 53 wt% grit, and between 67 and 84 % glycerine (each varied on its own).
  Expect 33 um/min, Ra 0.68 um, like single cream.
  Tightest limit: not too runny (margin +0.70).

--- Black silicon carbide 600 (d50 9.3 um) ---
For 100 ml of Black silicon carbide 600 slurry:
      51.6 g   grit
      34.6 ml  water
      49.2 ml  glycerine
         1     drop of dish soap
  (35 wt% solids, 64 wt% glycerine in the liquid)
  Still works between 23 and 47 wt% grit, and between 61 and 70 % glycerine (each varied on its own).
  Expect 22 um/min, Ra 0.32 um, like milk.
  Tightest limit: not too runny (margin +0.33).

--- Black silicon carbide 1000 (d50 4.5 um) ---
For 100 ml of Black silicon carbide 1000 slurry:
      81.1 g   grit
      52.9 ml  water
      21.8 ml  glycerine
         1     drop of dish soap
  (50 wt% solids, 34 wt% glycerine in the liquid)
  Still works between 48 and 54 wt% grit, and between 29 and 39 % glycerine (each varied on its own).
  Expect 12 um/min, Ra 0.15 um, like single cream.
  Tightest limit: will not crust (margin +0.08).

==========================================================================
  Black silicon carbide 600  on  Agate / chalcedony  over a Float glass plate
==========================================================================

SLURRY
  grit loading            34.8 wt%  = 16.1 vol%   (jams at 57 vol%)
  grain size             d50 9.3 um, top cut 19.0 um, Sauter mean 8.9 um
  liquid                 64 wt% glycerine, 5.8 x 10^-4 soap, mu = 14.1 mPa.s, gamma = 30 mN/m, a_w = 0.690
  slurry viscosity       27 mPa.s at high shear;  yield stress 0.0 Pa
  consistency            like milk   (gels above 27 vol% solids)
  blob height on plate   0.57 mm   <- measurable: leave a blob and see
  grit stays suspended   79 min

CONTACT  (regime: abrasive)
  pressure               100 kPa  (40 N over 400 mm2)
  plate gap              17.8 um  = 1.92 x d50
  load on grains         81 %   (rest is carried hydrodynamically by the film)
  grains actually cutting 5.24 per mm2  = 0.055 % of those present
  penetration into stone 499 nm at the deepest grain;  into the plate 689 nm
  grains being crushed   0.0 %
  grain anchoring        0.018  (grains rolling freely, three-body)

CUTTING
  stone removal          22.1 um/min   (brittle regime, 87 % of load cracking)
  plate wear             26.6 um/min   (1.20 x the stone)
  Preston coefficient    1.48e-11 m2/N
  drag                   9.2 N  (0.23 x the load); 2.3 W, contact +6 K

FINISH
  roughness              Ra 0.32 um, Rz 1.76 um
  subsurface damage      4.4 um   <- the next grit must remove at least this much
  worst-case scratch     0.57 um deep, from the 19 um top-cut grains

WORKING LIFE OF THE FILM ON THE PLATE
  8 ml over 200 cm2 = a 400 um film, losing 0.05 g water/min
  stays in the target band for indefinitely
  dries down to          20 vol% solids  (workable ceiling 52 vol%)
  - Cannot dry to a crust: glycerine holds the endpoint at 20 vol% solids, below the 52 vol% ceiling. It thickens and stops; a spray of water restores it.

No warnings: this recipe sits inside every target band.

==============================================================================
  WHAT THE FINER ABRASIVES WOULD ADD
==============================================================================

Grades finer than FEPA F1000, by micron size. They run in a smaller
gap, where the slurry film lifts harder.
Where the default technique will not carry them, the change that does
is given instead.

  abrasive                  grade    Ra um   SSD um   um/min  how to run it
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Green silicon carbide       3um    0.157     2.16      4.0  stroke slowly (0.06 m/s)
  Green silicon carbide       2um    0.052     0.72      0.6  stroke slowly (0.06 m/s)
  White fused alumina         3um    0.255     3.50     13.5  at the default stroke and pressure
  White fused alumina         2um    0.097     1.33      2.0  stroke slowly (0.06 m/s)
  White fused alumina         1um                             nothing in the list makes this work

==============================================================================
  HOW MUCH YOUR TARGETS MATTER
==============================================================================

The single most consequential setting is how thick 'not too runny' means.
Relaxing it lets you use far less glycerine:

     min low-shear viscosity  glycerine   grit   um/min
  --------------------------------------------------------
                        5 mPa.s        55 %    10 %     31.1
                       10 mPa.s        62 %    20 %     26.6
                       20 mPa.s        62 %    40 %     22.3
                       50 mPa.s        48 %    60 %     20.0

  The default of 20 mPa.s means 'clearly thicker than water, holds a
  film on a tilted plate'. Lower it if you are happy to stir often.
